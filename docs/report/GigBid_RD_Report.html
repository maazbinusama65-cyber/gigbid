Read this first. (1) The instructor's gigsim/, personas and hidden test set were not in the starter kit I received: gigsim/ is my own stand-in and all jobs, clients and gold labels are synthetic (some history titles and budgets come from a real Kaggle Upwork dump). (2) Every agent number uses the deterministic stub model profile (no LLM calls), so it tests routing, retrieval, policy, safety and plumbing, not the quality of LLM writing. 1. Problem and constraints

A freelancer on a marketplace spends hours scanning jobs, judging fit, pricing, and writing proposals that cost paid "Connects". GigBid scans, scores and drafts, then stops for a human decision. It must never: send a proposal or message, spend Connects, accept a contract, or touch identity or payment details without an explicit approval naming the exact draft; submit in live-marketplace mode at all; or follow instructions found inside a job post.

Declared budgets (agent/config.yaml): p95 latency 30 s per job analysed, cost $0.01 per job and $0.03 per proposal, 12,000 tokens per job, 30 graph steps, at most 2 Critic send-backs, a weekly Connects budget.

Why each agent exists. Analyst turns prose into a typed JobSpec; Risk Assessor reads client history and scam, free-work, off-platform and injection signals; Fit Matcher maps every requirement to portfolio evidence. They run in parallel through Send and share one deterministic requirement extractor so they stay independent. Decision applies thresholds from config. Writer, Verifier (every claim must cite evidence or is repaired), Critic (bounded loop) and Compliance Guard (marketplace policy) sit between the draft and the human. The conversation graph classifies client messages, drafts replies and sends only after approval.

Surfaces. One marketplace port with a GigSim adapter and a live-Upwork adapter (live mode never submits). Three own MCP servers expose 17 tools (Portfolio 3, Marketplace 11, Workspace 3) with schemas, read-only/destructive/idempotent hints, bearer auth, rate limits, idempotency keys and an audit log. Stores: LangGraph checkpointer (SQLite locally; a Postgres path exists but was not exercised), long-term memory with explicit promotion rules (style: 3 edits across 2 jobs; outcome: lift of 0.10 with 3 samples per side), a dedup store, JSONL traces, and the RAG indexes. Submit is exactly-once through a deterministic thread id plus the marketplace idempotency key. Not integrated: the official Upwork and GitHub third-party MCP servers. The Portfolio server is installable on its own (command in Appendix B).

3. R&D question A: tool granularity

Setup. 40 tasks, four tool surfaces: 35 one-endpoint tools with terse descriptions, 4 action-dispatch tools, the deployed 17-tool surface with rewritten descriptions, and the deployed surface with one-line descriptions as an ablation. Selection and arguments are scored by gold labels; tokens are the rendered schemas plus the task. The selector is an offline keyword-and-regex baseline, not an LLM, so this measures how lexically separable the tool sets are.

Claim verification

On 63 drafts that were given a fabricated client, year, metric or tool (63 each), the verifier detected 100% of every type and flagged 6.3% of true claims by mistake. Over 407 claims, unsupported claims fell from 99 to 0 after repair [rag_verify.json]. The stub writer composes drafts from evidence sentences, so its own unsupported-claim rate is near zero before verification; the fabricated-draft test above is the informative measure.

Pre-registered ablation hypothesis: In the best pipeline, removing requirement decomposition causes the largest Recall@5 drop of any single component; removing the cross-encoder reranker costs MRR/nDCG@10 more than Recall@5. FALSIFIED: the largest drop comes from removing skill graph fusion (-7.7 points), while removing decomposition changes Recall@5 by +0.6 (CI -1.5 to +2.5).

Not measured. The comparison needs a real tool-calling model on 50 dev jobs for a single ReAct agent, a langgraph-supervisor agent and the hand-built graph. 7. Signature extension X2: proposal A/B experiments

Two strategies are config, not code: A evidence_dense (up to 3 cited portfolio items) and B concise_question (one item, short approach, closing question). Both pass the same Verifier, Critic and Compliance Guard, so only style varies. 9. Safety and platform respect

Enforced: job text, screening questions and client messages are untrusted: delimited, injected sentences stripped, injected screening questions dropped, scam signals read from the stripped text; outgoing text passes the Compliance Guard and marketplace policy; submit_proposal and send_message are destructive-hinted and require an approval that names the draft; idempotency keys prevent double submits; secrets and PII are masked in traces; CI forbids committed secrets and inline prompts.

What still gets through, measured: scam recall on the held-out split is lowest for data_analyst at 0.75 (table in section 10), so some scams reach a draft and rely on the human approval. Limits of the injection result: attack success is 0 on the simulator's planted payloads, but these use a few templates and the stub profile has no LLM to hijack, so the 0 largely measures deterministic stripping, not an LLM's resistance. Before the fixes, planted payloads in screening questions reached the job spec (6 of 49 under the earlier, looser metric [decision_stub_before_fixes.json]); the metric was tightened afterwards to count only canary or $1-quote leakage, which is why the two figures are not directly comparable.
